Brian Turner · Project information

Multi-Tenant ATS Platform

The problem

Separate customer installations originated from the same core codebase but drifted apart. Fixes and improvements needed merging into individual systems. Some systems remained months or years behind, and functionality already built for one customer could require days of work to introduce elsewhere.

Rebuild and multi-tenant evolution

I defined the functionality and design for the ATS rebuild and its evolution into a multi-tenant platform, leading development, implementation and testing. The Core rebuild included a visual overhaul and substantial structural changes before the move to a shared application.

I conceived the shared platform approach and worked with the Technical Manager and Senior Engineer on its technical structure, detailed requirements and estimates. The platform used one application codebase with separate customer databases and shared services.

A configurable product

I developed the standard system specification and configurable functions with Sales, informed by market research. We aimed to standardise the offering around customer needs, providing a consistent experience and a competitive feature set for the price. The same platform could serve a fuller ATS or an entry-level application management offering for a different market and price point. Major functions contained further settings for individual behaviours, values and integrations.

Restricted configuration was managed through an existing central control panel used by me and two other senior staff. Content administration remained in the ATS. We adopted a standard managed colour scheme, with each customer retaining its logo.

Delivery and migration

A standard setup could be prepared from its URL, logo and email details. A separately ordered careers website followed a design and content process alongside ATS implementation.

Existing customers needed an assessment of their current functionality, agreed scope and a specified data migration. Tickets and Excel lists recorded what was included and excluded. I reviewed the tested implementation and directed further corrections.

Results and limitations

Standard setup and configuration reduced from days to hours, with wider delivery moving from months to weeks. These are recalled improvements rather than formally tracked measurements.

Shared application updates reached all customer systems, but database structures still needed scripted updates. Some implementations encountered issues because of inconsistencies in the source data being migrated. The platform was also continually evolving, and we did not have a fully established branching, merging and database version-control process to keep application changes and database updates aligned.

Releases were managed and scheduled to minimise disruption and downtime.
