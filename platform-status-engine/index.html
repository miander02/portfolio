Platform Status Engine

Platform operations, service protection & commercial visibility

This work brought together the health of shared recruitment services, the flow of applications into customer systems and the resources each customer consumed. It was designed to help staff understand what was running, where work was failing and what that activity cost the business.

The operational problem

An application could pass through job posting, email receipt, CV processing and candidate and job association before reaching the ATS. A failure in that chain could prevent applications becoming available to the customer. A service showing as running was not enough: we needed to see whether work was actually getting through.

Unexpected email volumes, repeated failures and retry loops also created operational and financial risk. Monitoring needed to identify the affected service and customer system, with controls to stop processing at either level.

My ownership

I defined the monitoring requirements and dashboard behaviour, working with engineering on service status, processing activity, queues, errors and per-system usage. I oversaw implementation and testing, and developed the requirements further as operational issues exposed gaps.

I wanted staff to be able to move from a platform-wide warning to the affected system and understand the failure, rather than investigate disconnected services without a clear view of the customer impact.

Protecting application delivery

The monitoring covered CV processing, application email processing, outgoing email, job posting and supporting services. Processing controls could operate across all systems or for an individual customer. Longer-term usage trends provided a reference for identifying excessive activity and developing protective responses.

A key distinction was between a service or ATS handoff failure and a source document that could not be extracted. Image-only or malformed CVs could be reported as errors even when the parser itself was running. Improving that classification was part of the development work.

Commercial purpose

Usage had a direct cost. Failed parser attempts consumed credits, and editing and resubmitting a job could consume another posting credit outside the applicable grace window. Successful CVs or jobs alone therefore did not show the full consumption.

I wanted usage by customer to support allowance management, identify unusually heavy consumption and provide evidence for pricing reviews. Monthly reports and exports supported operational and commercial review. The richer allowance and credit breakdowns in this sample illustrate that wider requirement.

Delivered and unfinished work

The service monitors, processing activity, usage reporting and controls formed the operational foundation. The original implementation was more basic than the broader management view I wanted.

The parser upgrade was not completed. Work to distinguish source-quality issues from service failures was being developed. The intended workflow to correct and resubmit posting errors or contact the customer from the error was also not completed. CRM alert links and browser notifications are shown here as proposed experiences.

About this sample

This fictional reconstruction demonstrates the operational and commercial behaviour in a modern interface. It uses generic service names and invented customers, figures, allowances and incidents. It does not connect to live services or calculate real customer charges.
